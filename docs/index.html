What the numbers support

Five readings, each tied to a figure above and to a column in the table below.

  • 1024 and 2048 tokens are one experiment, not two

    Hit rate, MRR, chunk count and index size are identical at both sizes in both retrievers, because SQuAD contexts fit in a single chunk from 1024 up (1.000 chunks/doc). The sweep has four distinct points, and nothing in it speaks to 2048.

  • Embeddings find more and rank worse

    Hit Rate@3 is higher at all 15 configurations (+2.0 to +6.0pp), while MRR@3 is lower in 11 of 15. The hit-minus-MRR gap averages 7.62pp for embeddings against 4.66pp for TF-IDF.

  • Overlap does nothing measurable here

    Holding chunk size fixed, the widest hit-rate spread between 0% and 20% overlap is 1.0pp in either retriever — two queries out of 200. The grid cannot tell these settings apart, so a 10% default is a free choice, not a finding.

  • No two configurations are statistically separated

    At n=200 a 95% Wilson interval runs ±3.1 to ±4.7pp. Every interval in the grid overlaps every other, the 92.5%/95.0% headline pair included. These are unpaired intervals and both retrievers answered the same questions, so McNemar on per-query outcomes would resolve far more — but per-query results were never saved, so it cannot be run from these files.

  • Cost is the one clean separation

    Embeddings take 5.3× longer to build the index (0.449 s against 0.085 s) and 38% less time per query (0.99 ms against 1.59 ms). TF-IDF query time tracks index size; the embedding retriever's does not.

Superseded run excluded

results.csv holds an earlier TF-IDF pass. It is kept in the repository and charted nowhere here, on two measurements: Hit Rate@3 and MRR@3 are identical in 15 of 15 rows, which requires every hit to land at rank 1; and 9 of 15 rows read exactly 1.000. The re-run in _run_tfidf_clean.py rebuilt chunking on LangChain's RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter and scores 0.925 where the old pass read 1.000. Its rows stay in the table below, struck through, so the record is complete rather than quietly tidied.
